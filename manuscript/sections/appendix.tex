\section{Implementation and selected settings}
\label{app:implementation}
The actor has 2,114 parameters. Initial weights are orthogonal, with gain $\sqrt{2}$ in hidden layers and $0.01$ in the action layer; biases start at zero. The world maps the outputs through tanh to a turn bounded by $\pi/4$ per step and a speed between zero and the body's maximum. Observations include clipped energy divided by birth energy and scaled body traits.

The per-step energy cost is $0.5+s^3v^2+0.05r$, where $s$ is size, $v$ is commanded speed after action squashing but before position clipping at a wall, and $r$ is sensing range. With the default body it is $1+v^2$. Ecological reproduction requires energy at least 200, costs the parent 120 and supplies the offspring with 100. Maximum age is 1,000 steps and the offspring placement offset is 1.

Within a step, agents move, consume reachable food, pay costs, die and reproduce; food dynamics then update. Random priority resolves contested food. Births require free slots, with eligible parents processed in random order, at most one child per parent per step, and payment only for successful births. At a varied-OE environment change, positions are clipped to the new arena as needed, while energy, age and genomes persist. Food is reinitialized under the new parameters.

Both PPO variants use 32 minibatches, GAE parameter 0.95, clipping parameter 0.2, value-loss coefficient 0.5, maximum gradient norm 0.5, initial log standard deviation zero and no learning-rate annealing. Table~\ref{tab:settings} gives the selected hyperparameters. Adaptation retains PPO's actor parameters, critic parameters, log standard deviation and reward-normalization state, while resetting its return accumulator, optimizer, environment state, counters and random stream. OE retains controller genomes and the alive mask, while resetting positions, headings, energy, age, actions and counters. ES retains its mean solution while restarting its optimizer, strategy and generation counter.

The PPO critic has the same two hidden layers as the actor and a scalar output. Exploration uses Gaussian actions with learned, state-independent log standard deviation. We use PureJaxRL commit \texttt{31756b1} and evosax version 0.3.2.

For PPO tuning, we sampled learning rate log-uniformly from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$, rollout length from $\{32,64,128\}$, update epochs from $\{2,4,8\}$, discount from $\{0.95,0.99,0.995\}$, entropy coefficient from $\{0,0.001,0.01\}$ and reward normalization on or off. For ES, we sampled perturbation scale from $[0.01,0.1]$ and learning rate from $[0.003,0.03]$, both log-uniformly, population size from $\{32,64,128\}$ and episodes per candidate from $\{1,2,4\}$. For OE, we sampled weight-mutation standard deviation log-uniformly from $[0.005,0.05]$, keeping the population floor and change interval fixed. Score ties favored the lower trial index.

Validation environments use seeds 4900--4919 plus four corner cases; T1, T2 and T3 use seeds 5900--5909, 5910--5919 and 5920--5929. Competence episodes use environment-specific evaluation seeds and checkpoint indices. Random streams are separated by purpose; shared seeds can couple corresponding runs across methods. The evaluation data include each environment's parameter values.

The OE--PPO-DR and ES-DR--ES-fixed adaptation comparisons use an interval rule with a margin of $0.05$. An interval wholly above zero supports the predicted direction; one wholly below zero opposes it. An interval between zero and $+0.05$, or between $-0.05$ and zero, is also below the practical margin. If neither directional condition holds, an interval contained in $[-0.05,+0.05]$ is labeled no meaningful difference; any other interval is inconclusive. Table~\ref{tab:tests} combines these intervals with the secondary rank-test results.

For the OE/PPO comparison, write $q_{ov},q_{of},q_{pv},q_{pf}$ for pooled AUC IQMs of OE, OE-fixed, PPO-DR and PPO-fixed. The prespecified summaries are
\begin{align}
 V&=\tfrac12[(q_{ov}-q_{of})+(q_{pv}-q_{pf})],\\
 M&=\tfrac12[(q_{ov}-q_{pv})+(q_{of}-q_{pf})],\\
 I&=(q_{ov}-q_{of})-(q_{pv}-q_{pf}),\qquad
 \Delta=|V|-|M|.
\end{align}
Here $V$ averages the two varied-minus-fixed contrasts, $M$ averages the two OE-minus-PPO contrasts, and $I$ measures their interaction (Table~\ref{tab:four}). The absolute-value contrast $\Delta$ is unstable when a component is close to zero.

\section{Supporting trait-only experiment}
\label{app:trait}
\subsection{Design and analysis}
The trait model combines inherited speed, size, sensing range and reach with fixed behavioral rules. The two phases contain 100 generations in environment A and 50 in B.

The groups are ET, the evolving population; F-food and F-pop, frozen copies of the food- and population-search winners; M-pop, mutating copies of the population-search winner; SV, a frozen initial population with standing variation; ET-freeze, the end-of-A evolved population with mutation disabled in B; ET-clone, copies of a randomly sampled end-of-A member with mutation disabled; and Oracle-B, the reference design selected directly in B. Oracle-B starts directly in B and is excluded from the confirmatory contrast family. Cloning changes the realized traits along with heterogeneity.

Each run seed defines its own searches and deployments, with 200 seeds per primary group and arm (2000--2199). Groups with the same seed share food, behavior and initial-position streams. The main B has 25 food items; three additional arms permit agent consumption in B, enlarge the B map to side 750, or reduce B's energy budget per generation to 25. We selected the larger-map and reduced-energy settings during pilot experiments before the corresponding final runs.

The generation rules derive from the MinuteLabs Evolution Simulator at commit \texttt{55af956} \citep{palfree_evolution_simulator}. A has side length 500 and 50 uniformly placed food items. Main B has side length 300 and 25 items in five clusters with standard deviation 15; centers and food are redrawn each generation. A generation lasts at most 200 ticks, with an energy allowance of 500 per agent except in the reduced-energy B arm. Agents start at edge home positions, search using fixed steering rules and return home according to feeding and remaining energy. Exhaustion and, in the consumption arm, predation can kill agents; agents with no food at generation end also die. Survivors carry into the next generation, and each survivor that consumed at least two food units produces exactly one offspring. Food units count both food items and prey. Effective movement speed is $u=\text{speed}\times\text{size}/10$, and each active movement tick costs $10^{-4}(\text{size}^3u^2+\text{sense})$. Food supports survival and reproduction without replenishing this generation-level energy allowance. There is no externally supported population floor.

Initial populations have 50 agents. ET starts from speed/size/sense/reach values $10/10/20/1$. Mutating offspring receive independent Gaussian trait perturbations with standard deviations $1/1/1/0.25$; sensing range is clipped at zero and the other traits at the smallest positive normalized float64 value. ET and M-pop mutate in both phases. F-food, F-pop, SV and Oracle-B have mutation disabled throughout. ET-freeze and ET-clone are derived from ET after A and have mutation disabled in B. SV samples each trait independently and log-uniformly over the search ranges: speed and size 2.5--40, sense 5--80 and raw reach 0.25--4.

In the consumption arm, a predator can consume only an agent whose size is at most 80\% of its own. Identical clones therefore cannot consume one another. Comparisons involving clones and heterogeneous populations also differ in access to this additional food source; differences among heterogeneous groups depend on their size distributions. Across all 50 B generations, mean prey counts per run are 150.8 for ET, 153.2 for M-pop, 18.4 for ET-freeze and 3.4 for SV, with zero for the four homogeneous frozen groups.

The A search begins with eight logarithmically spaced values for each trait. Merging designs with the same effective reach, $\max(\text{reach},\text{size}/4)$, leaves 1,472 distinct designs. Stage one evaluates 50 clones of each design for 30 generations on shared streams. Stage two evaluates the top 32 designs per objective on eight fresh sets of streams. In stage two, population search scores mean population over generations 10--99; food search scores food per agent over generations 10--29, even when a candidate was simulated for longer. The comparison therefore changes both objective and scoring horizon. Search-selected mutating agents inherit effective reach.

P1 is population at the start of a generation, averaged over B generations 40--49 with zeros retained. P2 indicates that no agent would start B generation 50. Each arm has eight planned comparison pairs, except the consumption arm omits the population-search versus food-search pair because those data repeat the main arm: 31 pairs in total. P1 uses a two-sided 10,000-permutation test of mean differences and a studentized bootstrap interval with 20,000 draws. P2 uses a two-sided Fisher test and a Newcombe interval for the difference in extinction proportions. Holm adjustment is within each arm's 16-test family, or 14 tests for the consumption arm. The tests treat groups as independent, although shared streams couple corresponding seeds.

The practical margins are 2.5 agents for P1 and 0.10 for P2. A ``meaningfully different'' label requires adjusted $p<0.05$ and an absolute point estimate at least as large as the margin, even if the interval crosses it. Equivalence requires an interval at level $1-2(0.05)/k$, where $k$ is the arm's family size, wholly inside the margins. Ceiling and floor frequencies show where boundary effects may mask population differences.

\subsection{Results}
In the main arm, F-pop reaches the population ceiling while ET is close to it. Their mean population difference is $0.6375$ against a $2.5$-agent margin, and neither group becomes extinct. F-pop also outperforms F-food (Figure~\ref{fig:trait}). Tables~\ref{tab:traitcontrasts} and~\ref{tab:traitgroups} give the contrasts and group outcomes.

In the larger-map condition, F-pop exceeds ET by $5.74$ agents (95\% interval $[5.00,6.66]$). Under reduced energy, ET-freeze has $3.93$ fewer agents than ET (interval $[1.66,6.12]$) and an extinction probability higher by $0.145$ (interval $[0.047,0.239]$).

F-food loses 62 of 200 main-arm runs during A; these runs contribute zeros to B outcomes. Restricting the comparison to populations that reach B would select survivors. The no-move control continues ET's end-of-A population for fifty further generations in A. Mean late population is 48.0675 with mutation, 47.9895 after freezing, and 40.9730 after cloning. The freeze-minus-mutation difference is $-0.0780$, interval $[-0.2720,0.1140]$; the clone-minus-freeze difference is $-7.0165$, interval $[-9.1271,-5.0470]$. Cloning therefore reduces population even without an environmental shift.

Searching directly in B improves population score by less than 2.5 agents over searching in A in 200 of 200 main-arm runs, 200 of 200 consumption-arm runs, 199 of 200 larger-map runs and 67 of 200 reduced-energy runs. The standing-variation group retains only one design at transfer in 153 of 200 runs, limiting its use as a diverse-population control.

In an exploratory 100-seed subset, starting ET directly in B instead of transferring from A changes P1 by $-0.22$ in the main arm, $-17.62$ in the larger-map arm and $-12.85$ in the reduced-energy arm.

\section{Protocols and reproducibility}
\label{app:reproduction}
The protocols were written before the corresponding runs but were not publicly registered. A three-seed pilot informed the choice of ten final runs per configuration.

The neural experiments use code revision \texttt{16e8657} for the five initial configurations and \texttt{06ff7b2} for ES-fixed. The trait experiment uses \texttt{a1e1cdc} for simulation and \texttt{561bcd4} for confirmatory analysis.

The initial neural runs used NVIDIA RTX 4090 hardware, JAX 0.11.2 and Python 3.13.18. Statistical reanalysis used Python 3.12.14 on CPU.

The released data contain sixty final neural evaluations, 19,800 adaptation checkpoint observations, trait trajectories, final policies, training snapshots and tuning results. Policies from the initial tuning runs were not retained. \texttt{REPRODUCE.md} gives the commands for the analyses, tables and figures.
